Who Owns the Digital Public Square? Social Media and the First Amendment

Writer: Gabriella Ruffels

Social media serves as a prominent venue for public discourse in the modern age. Through the sharing of images, videos, and bodies of text, public officials are connected to their constituents and held directly accountable for their choices while in office. These platforms – Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, and the like – facilitate public debate, yet they are controlled by private companies.

Biometric Authentication Under the Fifth Amendment

Writer: Gabriella Ruffels

The Fifth Amendment protects any citizen against being “compelled in any criminal case to be a witness against himself.” In other words, it prevents defendants from being coerced into providing incriminating, testimonial communications that could be used against them in court; however, it does not protect every piece of evidence that comes from a defendant.

Your Opinion Can Change the Constitution: How Public Perception Shapes the Cruel and Unusual Punishment Clause.

Writer: Lucas Kramer

The death penalty is as American as apple pie, and the U.S. Constitution, but why? The Eighth Amendment expressly prohibits “cruel and unusual punishments.” While some view capital punishment as an anachronistic practice that has no place in modern America, there are still others who endorse its continued use.
